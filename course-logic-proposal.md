# Course logic proposal — Applied Anomalology

Not implementation. No session/lecture/deck file is touched by this
document. This proposes how the course should hang together, using the
research already saved in `research/`, so the course logic can be settled
before `lecture-plan.md`'s per-week draft content gets worked through.

## 0. Design principle (new, settles a screening question raised mid-research)

**The course exists to surface the eeriness hidden beneath the surface of
ordinary life, for an audience of ordinary university students.** Not
exotic occultism, not secret-government-facility mythology, not
monster-of-the-week content. A case, tradition, or theory earns a place in
the course only if it's grounded in something an ordinary person could
plausibly brush up against: a threshold in their own home, a library, an
elevator, a dorm group chat, a VHS tape, a chain email, a big-box store
after closing. This doesn't change anything already decided — it
*explains* why several already-agreed calls are correct (open secret = the
phenomena themselves, not the department; no grand unifying theory; the
department's own voice stays bureaucratic-mundane, not mythic) — but it
does mean re-screening the research corpus, because some of it was
gathered before this was explicit.

### Ordinary-life audit of the sourced corpus

**Phase 1 — real traditions (items 1–5 in `sources.md`): all GOOD FIT.**
Iron/salt thresholds, fú talisman consecration, spirit walls, mezuzah
placement, ofuda/kamidana — every one of these is a real practice an
ordinary student could have seen in a relative's or a friend's home. This
is the strongest possible instantiation of the design principle: the
eeriness is *already* hidden in ordinary domestic life, no invention
needed. These should be the backbone of week 3, not a supporting citation.

**Phase 1 — fiction (items 6–12): mixed, and this is the one place the
audit changes a recommendation.**

| Item | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Ringu* (cursed tape) | GOOD FIT | An ordinary household media object. Keep as an adaptation source. |
| *House of Leaves* | GOOD FIT | An ordinary suburban house. Keep. |
| *Haunting of Hill House* | MODERATE | Domestic setting, but the "absolute reality" framing is a literary-psychological register more than an ordinary encounter. Fine as an assigned reading, not as an adaptation template. |
| *The Blair Witch Project* | MODERATE | Camping/woods is semi-ordinary; the escalating-marker *signal system* idea generalizes well to campus signage even if the setting doesn't. |
| "The Dunwich Horror" (Voorish Sign / Powder of Ibn-Ghazi) | **POOR FIT for adaptation** | Forbidden tome, invisible cosmic monster, exact occult recipe — this is exotic-occult, the opposite of the design principle. |
| "The Painted Skin" | **POOR FIT for adaptation** | A demon wearing human skin, a Taoist exorcist duel — mythological register, not ordinary life. |
| "Hōichi the Earless" | **POOR FIT for adaptation** | A ghost army of the dead, full-body sutra tattooing — same problem. |

**Recommendation, updated after the follow-up source search:** a real
replacement now exists for two of the three teaching points, so the
"keep all three, just relabel them" call above is superseded for two of
the three items. English witch bottles (`sources.md` Phase 1, item 6 of
the expansion pass — a real, still-attested two-stage diagnose-then-
neutralize practice with a documented partial-execution failure) cover the
Dunwich Horror detection/neutralization split with a better ordinary-life
fit than a forbidden tome and an invisible cosmic monster. Mezuzah fraud
(item 4a, extending the existing mezuzah tradition) covers the Painted
Skin object-substitution failure with a better fit than a demon-skin/
Taoist-exorcist duel, and adds a lesson Painted Skin didn't have
(a substitution failure invisible until tested). **Recommend dropping
Dunwich Horror and Painted Skin from the department's own procedure
teaching entirely, not just relabeling them** — the department's actual
countermeasure material can now be 100% real, ordinary practice for these
two failure modes, which is more coherent for a bureaucratic, ordinary-
life department than founding any part of its protocol on Necronomicon
or demon-skin lore, even as a labeled comparison. Hōichi the Earless
(the omission/incompleteness failure) has no equally strong real
replacement yet found — recommend keeping it as literary-comparison-only,
per the original call, pending a future search. Two new week-1/Phase-4
literary-comparison-only additions (Gilman's "The Yellow Wallpaper,"
*Lake Mungo*) can sit alongside Hill House in that same tier without
needing rehabilitation, since neither was ever proposed as an adaptation
template.

**Phase 2 (weeks 4–6, campus fieldwork): all 12 kept candidates are GOOD
FIT, no exceptions.** Elevators, dorm corridors, library time limits,
group chats, IT-support impersonation, a lights-out curfew — this is
already the strongest phase against the design principle, because it's
literally "things a SlopU student encounters in a normal week." No changes
recommended here; if anything, this phase should be the course's
centrepiece example of what "ordinary life eeriness" means, and week 1's
framing could say so explicitly.

**Phase 3 (weeks 7–8, off-campus desk study):** mostly GOOD FIT, one
flag.

| Item | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| SCP-3008 ("A Welcome IKEA") | GOOD | An ordinary big-box store. |
| SCP-173 | GOOD | A statue in mundane storage/facility conditions. |
| Smile Dog / smile.jpg | GOOD | A chain email/image forward — quintessentially ordinary digital life. |
| Kuchisake-onna | GOOD | Real documented urban panic; encountered on an ordinary street/school route. |

| LOCAL58 | GOOD | Broadcast television — an ordinary household medium made uncanny. |
| Ted the Caver | MODERATE | A hobby forum diary; caving isn't universal, but "an ordinary person's real-time online account" is a format worth keeping. |
| SCP-2317 ("A Door to Another World") | MODERATE | The door itself is ordinary; the far side (an extradimensional biome) isn't. Keep doorway-side framing, don't dwell on the far side. |
| SCP-093 ("Red Sea Object") | **WEAKEST FIT** | An archaeological relic — exotic-object horror, not ordinary life. |

**Recommendation, updated after the follow-up source search:** SCP-093 no
longer needs the "demote to single contrast case" hedge — a full
replacement pipeline now exists (`sources.md`'s "Week 7/8 supplement —
ordinary-life expansion pass" section), all GOOD FIT: Polybius (an arcade
cabinet), the Halloween-candy-tampering legend (real sociology
scholarship, Best & Horiuchi 1985), Petscop and BEN Drowned (secondhand
game media), and SCP-1048 "Builder Bear" (a stuffed toy) for week 7;
Bloody Mary, Hanako-san, and the Charlie Charlie Challenge for week 8 —
the Bloody Mary/Hanako-san pairing directly satisfies the site's own
week-8 spec line ("two cases from different countries... despite no
evident contact between their sources"). **Recommend dropping SCP-093
outright** rather than keeping it as a contrast case; the replacement set
is strong enough that a deliberately-weak example isn't needed to make
the point, and every other Phase 3 slot now has a same-or-better-fit
alternative available if the deck needs trimming.

**Phase 4 (frameworks, weeks 9–11): this is the *best* fit in the entire
corpus, and should be foregrounded, not buried as "literature review
content."** Freud's uncanny is literally the theoretical articulation of
this course's stated purpose (the frightening thing is "that class of the
frightening which leads back to what is known of old and long familiar").
Fisher's eerie is about failures of presence/absence in ordinary places.
Turner's liminality is about ordinary transitional spaces — doorways,
hallways, waiting rooms — which is also, not incidentally, exactly what
week 3's warding material protects. Brunvand's urban-legend scholarship
studies ordinary legends (hitchhiking, babysitting). Dégh & Vázsonyi and
Tolbert's ostension scholarship is about how ordinary communities turn
invented stories into treated-as-real local practice — directly explains
why a SlopU rule like "don't ride the elevator when it's already occupied"
would spread and stick. Kripal's fourth-dimension lineage is the one
moderately more exotic item (esoteric religious history), but it's useful
as context for why the trope exists, not as a claim the department makes.
**Recommendation: weeks 9–11 should open with this framing, explicitly**
— tell students in week 9 or 10 that the frameworks they're about to
compare all share one move, taking something ordinary and showing the
strangeness already inside it, and that this is the course's actual
thesis, stated once, not built toward a unifying answer.

**Update after the follow-up source search: two frameworks now have a
directly paired real case, not just an abstract citation.** Tolbert's
reverse-ostension paper can be taught alongside the real Slender Man
forum-thread origin and *Marble Hornets* web series (`sources.md` Phase 4
item 5) — deliberately as the real *process* the paper describes (a
photoshopped image, a home-video series), not as an import of the
creature itself into the department's case corpus, which stays excluded
per the Phase 3 audit above. Fisher's eerie can be taught alongside *The
Stone Tape* (item 6), which Fisher discusses by name as a worked example
— an ordinary old building and an ordinary recording technology, giving
students a concrete case the same week the concept is introduced, exactly
the case-to-concept pairing §3's Option C recommends. This also closes a
loop the original corpus left as fully abstract citations: two of the
five real Phase 4 frameworks now have a same-week worked case, not only
a name-check.

**Existing site content, re-checked against the same test:**

- Sub-basement library stacks (week 5): MODERATE — a library is ordinary,
  but "rearranges overnight" is an overused liminal-space trope without a
  specific ordinary hook. The gap-inventory's recommended replacement
  (SCP-2093, relocated to a disused seminar room) is also a *better*
  ordinary-life fit, not just a better rules-horror fit — two independent
  reasons to make that swap.
- 13-step stairwell (week 6): GOOD — a stairwell is about as ordinary as a
  space gets. Keep the setting, fix the missing edge case (see gap
  inventory / SCP-087 upgrade).
- "Cosmic prisoner theory" / "shadow domain theory" (week 10): fail on
  **two independent counts** — fabricated (theory audit) *and* poor fit
  (both read as exotic cosmic-containment mythology, closer to secret-
  organization fiction than to ordinary life). Replacing them with the
  real Phase 4 frameworks fixes both problems with the same edit.
- Fourth-dimensional hypothesis (week 11): MODERATE — keep it as the one
  contested view already agreed, but ground it explicitly in the real
  trope lineage (Kripal) rather than presenting it as the department's own
  physics, which is what currently makes it read as more exotic than it
  needs to.

## 1. Through-line: what a student can do after each phase

- **After Phase 1 (wk 1–3):** log → corroborate → classify an observation
  (method), and describe at least one real, ordinary threshold/protective
  practice with its actual procedure, sequence, limits, and a documented
  failure case — not "superstition," a defensible applied technique.
- **After Phase 2 (wk 4–6):** conduct supervised fieldwork under a safety
  protocol (consent / buddy / disengagement), produce a case file that
  states an anomaly's rule structure (trigger, edge case, escalation), and
  report whether a Phase-1 measure had any observable effect on it. This
  is where "applied" stops being a word in the course title and becomes
  something students actually did.
- **After Phase 3 (wk 7–8):** compare a campus case against a real,
  documented external corpus, and assess *sourcing quality* as its own
  skill (Backrooms Level 0's contested classification vs. SCP-2317's
  suspiciously over-solid official sourcing is a built-in teaching pair
  for exactly this).
- **After Phase 4 (wk 9–11):** evaluate competing real theoretical
  frameworks for explanatory power against the same case corpus, and
  articulate why the course doesn't pick one — because the frameworks
  disagree about mechanism while agreeing about the thing they're all
  trying to name.
- **After Phase 5 (wk 12):** synthesize all of the above into a proposed
  protocol for a novel case never seen before in the course.

### How this feeds the three assessments

- **Field Case File (wk 6, 30%):** needs weeks 4–6's rule-structured cases
  *and* week 3's procedural warding detail to exist, because the brief
  (case-file rigor, safety-protocol adherence, analytical quality) can't
  be met by a case with no rule and a countermeasure unit with no
  procedure to test against. Currently neither exists at the needed depth
  — this is the sharpest of the weak links below.
- **Comparative Literature Review (wk 9, 30%):** needs an actual named
  case corpus (Phase 3) and at least the beginning of theoretical framing
  (Phase 4 pulled slightly earlier, or referenced ahead) to compare
  against. Currently the corpus is entirely absent (gap inventory: "zero
  actual cases named").
- **Final Capstone (wk 12, 40%):** needs everything above to be real,
  since it asks students to draw on classification + countermeasure
  protocol structure + fieldwork case-writing + theoretical evaluation
  simultaneously. It's the assessment most exposed by every other phase's
  current thinness.

## 2. Weak links (specific, not general)

1. **Weeks 5–6 test "did a warding measure have any effect," but week 3
   currently has zero concrete procedure to test.** Fix order matters:
   week 3 must be built (using Phase-1 real-tradition sources) *before*
   weeks 5–6 can meaningfully reference it, or the fieldwork phase is
   testing something that was never defined.
2. **Week 4's buddy-system rule has nothing that requires it.** SCP-2093's
   external-rescue mechanic (only a second person outside can open the
   door) is the one sourced case that gives the buddy system real
   narrative teeth — currently unconnected to any case.
3. **Week 9's "does it converge on one cause" lecture presupposes named
   cases from weeks 7–8 that don't exist yet.** Right now it's a lecture
   about convergence with nothing to converge.
4. **Week 10's frameworks need to exist before week 11 can meaningfully
   argue between them.** Currently week 11 is built entirely on week 10's
   fabricated material, so fixing week 10 is a precondition for week 11,
   not a parallel, independent fix.
5. **Week 3's warding material never reappears after week 3** in the
   current site — the countermeasure step of the department's own
   observe → classify → countermeasure method is the least-exercised of
   the three past week 3. Weak links 1 and 2 above are specific instances
   of this general gap; fixing them together (rather than one at a time)
   is what actually closes it.

## 3. Alternative progression structures

**Option A — keep the current phase order, fill it with real sourced
content.** Foundations → On-campus → Off-campus → Synthesis → Capstone,
unchanged week ranges and assessment dates.
*Trade-off:* lowest disruption — the existing narrative logic (learn
method+countermeasures, then practice locally, then widen to a global
corpus, then theorize, then synthesize) is already defensible and matches
locked-in assessment due dates. Downside: still front-loads three weeks of
method/theory before students see a single interesting case, which risks
feeling like paperwork if weeks 1–3 aren't written with enough hook
material (the Phase-1 fiction sources, used as comparison reading rather
than lecture filler, help with this).

**Option B — open with a hook case, then formalize method.** Introduce one
strong ordinary-life case (e.g. the elevator or dorm-door rule) briefly in
week 1–2 as a motivating example, *before* the formal observation protocol
is taught, then return to the same case with method applied in weeks 5–6.
*Trade-off:* more narratively engaging opening — start with the eerie
thing, not the paperwork. But it cuts against the department's own stated
logic that procedure comes first (a case is currently introduced only
after weeks 1–2 establish how to log one responsibly), and it touches the
week 1–2 session content, which the gap inventory otherwise rates as
solid and doesn't recommend changing.

**Option C — merge desk study and theory into one paired phase.** Instead
of "here are cases" (wk 7–8) then, three weeks later, "here are theories"
(wk 10–11), pair one real case with the theoretical frame that explains it
best, per week (e.g. wk 7: Backrooms Level 0 / SCP-173 paired with
Fisher's eerie; wk 8: Kuchisake-onna paired with Brunvand + ostension
theory). Week 9 keeps its existing convergence lecture, now with material
to converge across.
*Trade-off:* tighter case-to-concept coupling, and directly strengthens
the Literature Review (which needs both a corpus and theoretical
evaluation, currently taught three weeks apart). Costs: a bigger structural
change to weeks 10–11's shape (no longer "competing frameworks, then
current state of the field" as two distinct movements), and less room for
a dedicated side-by-side case-comparison lecture, since comparison and
theory are now merged per week rather than sequential.

**Recommendation (not a decision):** Option A's shape, with Option C's
case-theory pairing folded into how weeks 9–11 are *written* rather than
restructuring which weeks exist — keep the phase/assessment boundaries
exactly where they are, but stop treating "here are the cases" and "here
are the frameworks" as two unrelated movements three weeks apart.

## 5. Decisions (settled 2026-09-21)

1. **Progression: A+C blend** — keep the existing phase/week/assessment
   boundaries exactly where they are (Option A); fold Option C's
   case-theory pairing into how weeks 9–11 are *written*, not into a
   restructure of which weeks exist. This is the recommendation from §3,
   now confirmed as the direction, not just a suggestion.
2. **Confirmed.** Dunwich Horror and Painted Skin are dropped outright
   from the department's own procedure teaching (witch bottles and
   mezuzah fraud supersede their teaching roles). Hōichi the Earless stays
   literary-comparison-only until a real omission-failure replacement
   turns up.
3. **Confirmed.** SCP-093 (Red Sea Object) is dropped outright — no
   "contrast case" hedge. The full week 7/8 replacement pipeline (Polybius,
   Halloween-candy legend, Petscop, BEN Drowned, SCP-1048; Bloody Mary,
   Hanako-san, Charlie Charlie Challenge) stands as its replacement.
4. **Confirmed: name real traditions explicitly**, as themselves (fú
   talismans, mezuzah, ofuda, witch bottles, nazar, etc.) rather than
   using dry in-universe renaming ("Class II boundary reinforcement"). The
   department cites real sources directly in week 3, in character — this
   also matches the design principle (§0): the point is that these are
   things an ordinary person could already recognize, which the
   department's own renaming would otherwise obscure.
5. **Confirmed.** Library sub-basement (wk 5) is replaced by the
   SCP-2093-derived seminar-room case; the stairwell (wk 6) keeps its
   setting but gains the SCP-087-derived edge case.
6. (a) **Trimmed, not all three included.** Witch balls are cut (weakest
   fit — no documented failure case, and no case anywhere in the corpus
   that exercises it). Nazar amulets and mirror-covering during shiva are
   kept: nazar is the one measure weeks 5–6 fieldwork can directly test
   (see `lecture-plan.md`'s cross-reference table), and mirror-covering is
   the direct real-world tie-in for the Tolbert/Slender-Man discussion in
   week 9. Final week-3 tradition count: 5 original + witch bottles +
   mezuzah fraud + nazar + mirror-covering = **9**.
   (b) **Confirmed.** Tolbert/Slender-Man-origin+*Marble Hornets* and
   Fisher/*The Stone Tape* pairings both approved for weeks 9–10; the
   Slender Man creature itself stays excluded from the case corpus.
   (c) **Confirmed.** "Candle Cove" is the week 1 framing hook.

**Status: course logic settled.** Next step is phase-by-phase work through
`lecture-plan.md`'s draft content, starting with Phase 1 (weeks 1–3).
