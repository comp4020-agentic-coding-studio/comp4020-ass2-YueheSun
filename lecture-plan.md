## Per-week breakdown (all 12 weeks)

### Week 1 — Foundations: course overview & method (existing lecture+deck)
- **Role:** orient students to the department's method and the course's
  actual thesis, before any case is logged.
- **Points:** (1) anomalology as an applied observational method, not a
  belief system; (2) the observe → classify → apply-countermeasure loop;
  (3) the sourcing standard, previewed: a case must describe behaviour,
  not atmosphere, and must be corroborated; (4) the course's design thesis
  stated once, plainly — the eeriness this course studies is already
  hidden under the surface of ordinary life, not imported from anywhere
  exotic.
- **Sourced case / hook:** "Candle Cove" (Kris Straub, 2009) `[SETTLED
  Q6c: confirmed]` — an in-universe forum thread where students "remember"
  a campus event official records show never happened. Opens on collective
  false memory as a phenomenon worth logging carefully, before any warding
  material is introduced.
- **Assessment link:** sets the documentation-rigor standard the Field
  Case File (wk 6) is later marked against.

### Week 2 — Foundations: classification scheme & historical framing
- **Role:** formalize the department's classification vocabulary before
  it's used on real cases. Existing practical: "Historical taxonomies of
  anomalous phenomena"; no lecture yet.
- **Points:** (1) the four-category scheme — persistent / recurring /
  responsive / dormant — introduced formally, non-exclusive by design;
  (2) why non-exclusive matters, previewed with SCP-1048's persistent-
  plus-dormant profile (full case held for week 7); (3) the department's
  practice of describing traditions generically rather than via named
  historical incidents, and why (the same rule this course's own sourcing
  follows).
- **Gap, flagged honestly:** nothing in the gathered corpus targets week 2
  specifically — every Phase 1 item is tagged for week 3. Recommend either
  a short follow-up search for a real historical-taxonomy source (distinct
  from Brunvand/Dégh-Vázsonyi, which are held for week 9), or building the
  week entirely around applying the four-category scheme retroactively to
  cases students will meet properly later — not inventing an unsourced
  example to fill the slot.
- **Assessment link:** the classification scheme is the rubric backbone
  both the Field Case File (wk 6) and the Capstone (wk 12) require
  students to apply.

### Week 3 — Foundations: comparative warding (existing lecture+practical)
- **Role:** protective practice taught as applied research, not
  superstition — the department's actual toolkit.
- **Points:** (1) five original real traditions (iron/salt thresholds, fú
  talismans, spirit walls, mezuzah placement, ofuda/kamidana/shimenawa) as
  applied research, each with real procedure, sequence, limits, and a
  documented failure case; (2) `[SETTLED Q6a: trimmed]` four supplementary
  traditions become **three** — English witch bottles, mezuzah fraud, nazar
  amulets, and mirror-covering during shiva. Witch balls are cut (weakest
  fit — no documented failure case, no case anywhere in the corpus that
  exercises it). Final count: 9 traditions; (3) `[SETTLED Q2: confirmed]`
  the two failure-mode fiction comparisons are now taught as **real
  practice**, not fiction: witch bottles for the detection/neutralization
  split (replacing Dunwich Horror, dropped outright), mezuzah fraud for the
  object-substitution failure (replacing Painted Skin, dropped outright);
  Hōichi the Earless stays as literary-comparison-only, since no real
  omission-failure replacement has been found yet; (4) `[SETTLED Q4:
  confirmed]` every tradition is named explicitly as itself (fú talisman,
  mezuzah, witch bottle, etc.), in character — the department does not use
  dry in-universe renaming for this material, since the point is that
  students can recognize these as things they could plausibly encounter.
- **Sourced case:** the full practical-measures table in `sources.md`.
- **Assessment link:** this is the vocabulary weeks 5–6's fieldwork tests
  the effect of, and the procedural language the Capstone protocol design
  reuses. See the cross-reference table below — several measures don't yet
  have a campus case that tests them, flagged there rather than papered
  over here.

### Week 4 — On-campus fieldwork: method & safety protocol (existing)
- **Role:** fieldwork method — consent, buddy system, disengagement rule
  — before any case clinic.
- **Points:** (1) the buddy-system/disengagement procedure, sourced from
  `sources.md`'s existing "Real-world safety-protocol material"; (2)
  **fixes weak link #2** (`course-logic-proposal.md` §2): preview SCP-2093
  here as the reason the buddy system exists — its external-rescue
  mechanic is the first case in the corpus that actually *requires* the
  rule, rather than the rule sitting unmotivated until week 5.
- **Sourced case:** SCP-2093 (previewed), existing safety-protocol
  material.
- **Assessment link:** the Field Case File's "safety-protocol adherence"
  criterion is graded against exactly this week's material.

### Week 5 — On-campus fieldwork: case clinic 1 (existing, content swap)
- **Role:** first live case clinic, applying the classification scheme.
- **Swap, confirmed** `[SETTLED Q5]`: replace the library sub-basement

- **Points:** (1) applying the four-category scheme to a live-feeling

### Week 7 — Off-campus desk study: regional survey (existing, content added)
- **Role:** corroboration and sourcing-quality method, applied to a wider
  corpus.
- **Points:** (1) the three-way sourcing-failure-mode teaching triad:
  SCP-2317 (over-solid-but-suspicious official sourcing), Smile Dog
  (structurally uncorroborable), Polybius (zero surviving evidence — new
  from the expansion pass); (2) case-format literacy across text
  (SCP-173, SCP-1048) and video/software claims (Petscop, BEN Drowned);
  (3) legend-as-real-defensive-behaviour, via the Halloween-candy-
  tampering scholarship (Best & Horiuchi 1985), bridging toward Brunvand
  and Dégh/Vázsonyi's full treatment in week 9.
- **Sourced cases:** SCP-173, SCP-1048, Polybius, Halloween-candy legend,
  Petscop, BEN Drowned. BEN Drowned's "the obvious fix makes it worse"
  lesson cross-references week 3's countermeasure material directly.
- **Assessment link:** feeds the Comparative Literature Review (due wk 9).

### Week 8 — Off-campus desk study: global survey (existing, content added)
- **Role:** cross-cultural comparison — the week's own spec line ("two
  cases from different countries... despite no evident contact").
- **Points:** (1) Bloody Mary / Hanako-san as the textbook pairing that
  satisfies that spec line directly — same responsive-ghost-in-an-
  institutional-fixture structure, independently documented, no plausible
  transmission between them; (2) Freud's uncanny previewed concretely via
  Bloody Mary's own scholarly finding (participants report their own
  reflection, reframed); (3) the Charlie Charlie Challenge as a live

  older regional tradition.
- **Sourced cases:** Bloody Mary, Hanako-san, Charlie Charlie Challenge,
  Kuchisake-onna, Backrooms Level 0, LOCAL58, Ted the Caver.
- **Assessment link:** feeds the Comparative Literature Review.

### Week 9 — Off-campus desk study: convergence (existing lecture+practical)
- **Role:** **fixes weak link #3**: the convergence lecture now has
  material and frameworks to converge with, instead of presupposing named
  cases and theories that didn't exist yet.
- **Points:** (1) reframe "does it converge on one cause?" honestly as
  "no — and here's the method for saying so responsibly," using Brunvand's
  urban-legend method and Dégh & Vázsonyi's ostension typology as the
  actual analytical tools, not just cited names; (2) `[SETTLED Q6b:
  confirmed]` Tolbert's reverse-ostension paper paired with the real Slender Man
  forum-origin/*Marble Hornets* case — does the department's own corpus
  look like documented ostension or manufactured reverse ostension? The
  creature itself stays excluded from the case corpus; only the real
  forum-thread/web-series process is used.
- **Sourced case:** the full week 7–8 corpus, plus the Tolbert pairing.
- **Assessment link:** **Comparative Literature Review due (30%).**

### Week 10 — Literature synthesis: competing frameworks (existing, content replaced)
- **Role:** replace the two fabricated frameworks ("cosmic prisoner
  theory," "shadow domain theory") with real, citable ones, each paired
  with a case students have already seen.
- **Points:** (1) Freud's uncanny, paired with Bloody Mary and Candle
  Cove; (2) Fisher's weird/eerie, paired `[SETTLED Q6b: confirmed]` with
  *The Stone Tape* and Backrooms Level 0; (3) Turner's liminality, paired
  with
  mirror-covering during shiva (week 3 cross-reference) and the
  threshold/doorway cases already seen; (4) the course's actual thesis
  stated explicitly: these frameworks share one move — taking something
  ordinary and showing the strangeness already inside it — not a single
  unifying explanation.
- **Assessment link:** feeds the Capstone's evaluation criterion.

### Week 11 — Literature synthesis: current state of the field (existing lecture)
- **Role:** replace the invented "unifying fourth-dimensional hypothesis"
  while keeping the same narrative slot — one leading-but-contested view,
  open dissent.
- **Points:** (1) Kripal's fourth-dimension trope lineage taught
  explicitly as real trope history, not the department's own physics;
  (2) Cho & Underberg-Goode's 2025 Backrooms scholarship as a worked
  example of citing a real published source against a live case, modeling
  the Comparative Literature Review's own citation standard; (3) open
  dissent stated as the course's actual position, not a cliffhanger —
  no week-10 framework is declared the winner.
- **Assessment link:** feeds the Capstone.

### Week 12 — Capstone: final debrief (existing practical, co-taught)
- **Role:** synthesis. Design a containment/warding protocol for a novel
  case, grounded in the classification scheme (wk 2) and countermeasure
  vocabulary (wk 3).
- **Points:** (1) protocol design task, marked holistically; (2) the 8
  capstone idea seeds in `sources.md` offered as optional starting
  prompts, not mandatory templates; (3) co-taught review of the method
  end-to-end (observe → classify → apply countermeasure → write up →
  compare → theorize → protocol).
- **Assessment link:** **Final Capstone due (40%).**
