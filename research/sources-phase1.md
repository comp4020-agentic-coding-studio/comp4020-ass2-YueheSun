# Phase 1 source research

Scope: source material for SlopU Phase 1 (weeks 1–3, "theory + warding
traditions, East and West"), gathered by web search on 2026-09-21. This
builds directly on `research/01-theory-audit.md`, which already established
that fú (符) talismans, door-as-Qi-mouth placement, and spirit walls (影壁)
are real and citable, that Western iron/salt threshold magic is real
(apotropaic magic, tied to Mary Douglas on purity/boundaries and Victor
Turner on liminality), and that "feng shui boundary theory" as a named
umbrella is the course's own coinage, not a term practitioners use. Nothing
below re-derives that verdict; it extends the same material with more
procedural detail and adds fiction/film sources that dramatize
countermeasures with explicit rules. No source below involves a real named
private individual, a real death, or an identifiable real institution or
incident — all real-tradition material is generic/traditional, and all
narrative material is published fiction or film.

### Real traditions (procedural detail)

#### 1. European apotropaic magic — iron and salt at thresholds

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apotropaic_magic ;
  https://crookedkeysociety.com/protective-magic-folklore-iron-salt/ ;
  https://spiralrain.ca/blogs/blog-posts/iron-salt-and-rowans-traditional-folk-charms-for-home-protection
- **Author/site:** Wikipedia (CC BY-SA); Crooked Key Society and Spiral Rain
  (independent folklore blogs, standard copyright, no reuse license stated)
- **Language:** English
- **Week fit:** 3 (Western half of the comparative unit)
- **Tags:** apotropaic magic, iron, salt, threshold, folk magic, boundary

Apotropaic magic is the scholarly umbrella term for magic that repels
rather than summons — practiced at the "precarious passages" of birth,
sleep, death, and doorways, where identity or safety is considered to blur.
Concrete procedure: iron nails (sometimes forged with exactly three hammer
strikes) driven into door frames, window sills, hearthstones, or the four
corners of a property; horseshoes nailed above doors; salt sprinkled across
the inside of doorways, windowsills, and room corners. A documented
combination object is the witch bottle/charm bag — salt plus iron nails
plus herbs, buried under the threshold or hidden in a chimney, meant to
trap or reflect harmful magic sent at the house. A documented limit: salt
dissolves in rain and gets tracked away by foot traffic, which is the
stated folk reason iron (permanent) and salt (temporary, needs renewal) are
paired rather than used alone. Teachable concept: protection scaled to
where a boundary is physically crossed, with an explicit failure mode
(perishable material) driving the choice of a backup material.
**SlopU adaptation:** frame as "Class II boundary reinforcement," the
department's dry name for a real, still-practiced folk technique — a
running joke where bureaucratic renaming is the horror-comedy device, not
the material.

#### 2. Fú (符) talisman consecration procedure

- **URL:** https://www.taoismandmanifest.com/blogs/all/fu-talisman-taoist-paper-charm-home ;
  https://longhumountain.com/blogs/introduction-to-taoism-q-a/the-steps-for-drawing-talismans-%E9%81%93%E6%95%99%E7%94%BB%E7%AC%A6%E6%AD%A5%E9%AA%A4 ;
  https://www.kaucim.ai/en/articles/taoist-talisman-meaning
- **Author/site:** Independent Taoism-practice/education sites; standard
  copyright, no reuse license stated
- **Language:** English (translated from Chinese-language practice
  literature)
- **Week fit:** 3
- **Tags:** fú talisman, Taoism, consecration, cinnabar, ritual sequence,
  kai guang

Extends what the theory audit already confirmed (fú are real, priest-
consecrated paper charms). The fuller procedure has four distinct,
sequential stages, and sources are explicit that skipping one produces "an
approximation," not a functioning fú: (1) purification/invocation — incense,
prayer, naming the petitioner and the presiding deities; (2) drawing —
performed in one continuous, unbroken brushstroke in cinnabar ink on yellow
paper, since a pause is said to break the flow of qi from priest to page,
accompanied by silent incantation and hand seals; (3) sealing — a lineage
temple stamp in cinnabar authenticates the object; (4) consecration/
activation ("kai guang," opening of light) — water or ritual-fire rites
that make the talisman actually efficacious rather than decorative. Some
traditions specify timing (11pm–1am) and checking the petitioner's year
against the presiding Tai Sui deity. Teachable concept: a ritual object
with a documented multi-step manufacturing pipeline, each step load-bearing
— directly useful as a template for an in-universe "countermeasure
protocol" with numbered steps and stated failure conditions.
**SlopU adaptation:** the department's "standard countermeasure protocol"
template (numbered steps, a materials list, a stated failure mode for
skipping any step) can be modeled almost directly on this real four-stage
structure, credited as inspiration rather than presented as the real
practice.

#### 3. Spirit walls (影壁/照壁) and the door as "mouth of Qi"

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_screen ;
  https://fengshuinexus.com/feng-shui-rules/feng-shui-rules-related-to-supernatural/ ;
  https://theqiflow.com/blogs/feng-shui/feng-shui-front-door-opens-to-a-wall
- **Author/site:** Wikipedia (CC BY-SA); independent feng shui practice
  sites, standard copyright
- **Language:** English
- **Week fit:** 3
- **Tags:** spirit wall, yingbi, zhaobi, qi mouth, straight-line ghosts,
  poison arrow

A spirit wall (yingbi, or zhaobi when placed outside the gate) is a real
screen placed directly in front of a temple, sacred precinct, or house
entrance. Its stated dual purpose is architectural privacy (blocking the
view of passersby) and a specific supernatural claim: ghosts are believed
to travel only in straight lines and cannot turn a corner, so a wall placed
opposite the gate physically defeats their approach. The same "no straight
lines" logic governs the door itself, called the "mouth of Qi" (氣口) in
classical feng shui — a straight, high-speed approach path from a road to
the door is a "poison arrow," remedied by low shrubs, a large stone, or a
curved path breaking the line. A separate, distinct concept is the
"ghost-gate" (鬼门), a fixed compass direction (northeast/Gen trigram) where
spirits are believed to pass, independent of any specific building's
layout. Teachable concept: a protective measure derived directly from a
stated mechanical limitation of the threat (can't turn corners), which is
a clean, testable "rule" in the sense a horror-mechanic needs one.
**SlopU adaptation:** a numbered "Directive: no unbroken sightline greater
than N metres between an exterior opening and an occupied room" — an
in-universe safety code visibly descended from spirit-wall logic without
naming feng shui as its source.

#### 4. Mezuzah placement (Jewish practice)

- **URL:** https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/225405/jewish/Mezuzah-Placement.htm ;
  https://www.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/278460/jewish/How-to-Hang-a-Mezuzah.htm
- **Author/site:** Chabad.org (Chabad-Lubavitch Media Center); standard
  copyright
- **Language:** English
- **Week fit:** 3 (third comparative tradition)
- **Tags:** mezuzah, doorpost, halacha, precise placement, threshold law

Not primarily a warding-against-ghosts practice, but included because it is
the most exactingly *procedural* threshold practice found: a scroll is
sealed in a case and fixed to the doorpost (never the door itself, which
moves), on the right side as a person enters, angled with the top tilted
inward, at a specific measured height — the bottom of the case aligned two-
thirds up the doorpost, but no lower than roughly shoulder height on an
unusually tall doorway, and no higher than about 4 inches below the lintel.
If the doorpost is wider than a handbreadth, the mezuzah is mounted in the
outermost handbreadth, not centred. Ambiguous interior doorways (which side
is "entering") require a specific ruling rather than guessing. A blessing
is recited immediately before fixing it, with no conversation in between.
Teachable concept: a real tradition where *precision of placement*, not
just presence of the object, is treated as the difference between
efficacious and inert — good comparative counterpoint to the fú talisman's
precision-of-procedure.
**SlopU adaptation:** a running gag/rule that the department's own
placement tolerances for a ward ("no lower than X, no higher than Y from a
measured datum") are lifted wholesale from a real precision-placement
tradition, restated as if independently derived by committee.

#### 5. Ofuda, kamidana, and shimenawa (Japanese Shinto household warding)

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofuda ; https://kamidana.app/journal/en/ofuda-guide ;
  https://japaninsidersecrets.com/ofuda/
- **Author/site:** Wikipedia (CC BY-SA); independent Shinto-practice sites,
  standard copyright
- **Language:** English
- **Week fit:** 3 (fourth comparative tradition, if used)
- **Tags:** ofuda, kamidana, shimenawa, shide, household ward, renewal cycle

Ofuda are shrine-issued paper/wood talismans naming an enshrined deity and
stamped with the shrine's seal, placed high on a north or west wall or in a
household altar (kamidana). Placement has a documented order when three
ofuda are used together: in a single-doored altar they are stacked, with
the Ise/Amaterasu talisman on top (frontmost); in a three-doored altar the
Amaterasu talisman takes the centre door, the ancestral shrine's the right,
the local shrine's the left. Shimenawa (twisted rope) with shide (paper
zigzag streamers) mark an object or space as purified and warded, hung
across the front of the altar space. Specialised ofuda go to specific
rooms tied to their function (a hearth deity's ofuda in the kitchen; a
purifying deity's talisman in the toilet). Critically, ofuda are not
permanent: they are returned to the shrine and replaced annually, typically
in December — a stated renewal requirement, not a one-time installation.
Teachable concept: a warding object with an explicit expiry/renewal cycle,
distinct from the "install once" logic of iron or a spirit wall.
**SlopU adaptation:** the department's countermeasures could have a
"shelf life" field on their equipment forms — wards that must be reissued
annually or their protection lapses silently, mirrored on the real ofuda
renewal custom.

### Fiction and film with explicit countermeasure rules

#### 6. *Ringu* (novel 1991, Kōji Suzuki; film 1998, dir. Hideo Nakata) — the cursed tape

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_(film) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_(novel_series) ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ring_Virus
- **Author/site:** Wikipedia (CC BY-SA), Ring franchise wikis (fan-run,
  unclear license)
- **Language:** English (source is Japanese)
- **Week fit:** 3
- **Tags:** cursed object, rule + loophole, forced complicity, media horror

The rule is stated plainly in-universe: anyone who watches the cursed
videotape dies seven days later (in the novel, via a throat tumor). The
only documented countermeasure is to copy the tape and show it to someone
else within the seven days — made fully explicit as a rule in the Korean
adaptation *The Ring Virus*. Critics have noted the "loophole" is not
really an escape: it converts every survivor into someone who has to
inflict the same curse on a new, unwitting victim to live, making the tape
function like a self-propagating contagion (a metaphor repeatedly read
onto the VHS format itself). No character is shown finding any way to
break the cycle rather than extend it. Teachable concept: a countermeasure
that "works" mechanically but is morally compromised — useful for showing
that a documented, reliable procedure is not automatically an ethically
clean one, and that a "protective measure" can just relocate a problem.
**SlopU adaptation:** an in-universe artifact whose official countermeasure
form requires the technician to log who they passed the risk to — turning
the department's own paperwork into the site of horror.

#### 7. *House of Leaves* (Mark Z. Danielewski, 2000) — the house's spatial rules

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Leaves ;
  https://www.rootsimple.com/2024/10/the-spatial-horror-of-house-of-leaves/
- **Author/site:** Wikipedia (CC BY-SA); Root Simple (independent blog,
  standard copyright)
- **Language:** English
- **Week fit:** 1 or 2 (theory framing, ahead of the week 3 countermeasure
  unit)
- **Tags:** non-Euclidean space, measurement failure, unmappable, found
  footage

A family's house is measured and found to be a quarter-inch larger inside
than outside; a hallway and an expanding labyrinth subsequently appear
where there was previously a blank wall. The house has no attacks, no
visible monster, and does not "want" anything legible — its horror is
that it defeats measurement and mapping outright: an explorer's own chalk
trail-markings vanish behind him as he walks. Analysis frames this as
horror of pure unmappable space rather than a threat with rules to learn,
and notes the book's own typography (rotated text, fragmenting layout,
proliferating footnotes) formally mirrors the house's instability — form
enacting the content. Teachable concept: an anomaly defined by the absence
of a stable rule set is itself a category the department needs a name for
("non-compliant geometry," e.g.) — a useful contrast case before week 3
introduces measures that presuppose the anomaly obeys fixed rules.
**SlopU adaptation:** a case file the department admits it cannot write a
standard countermeasure for, used to justify why the countermeasure unit
opens with cases that *do* have rules (Western/Chinese warding) rather than
this one.

#### 8. *The Haunting of Hill House* (Shirley Jackson, 1959) — opening frame

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Haunting_of_Hill_House
- **Author/site:** Wikipedia (CC BY-SA); novel itself, Viking Press, 1959
- **Language:** English
- **Week fit:** 1
- **Tags:** absolute reality, sanity, ambiguity, haunting as psychology

The novel's opening paragraph ("Hill House, not sane, stood by itself
against its hills...") frames the haunting not as an intrusion of the
supernatural into a stable world, but as unmediated "absolute reality"
itself being what no organism — human, insect, or building — can endure
without dreaming/distortion as a defense. Commentary highlights the
ambiguity Jackson deliberately preserves throughout: whether the house
possesses its occupants or an occupant's own instability is what animates
the house is never resolved. No character in the book has a working
countermeasure; the closest thing to a "rule" is that the house responds
to the personalities and histories of who is inside it. Teachable concept:
a theoretical frame (an anomalous site as revealing rather than intruding)
that is useful to introduce in week 1 before the countermeasure material
in week 3, since it sets up "sometimes there is no procedure, only
exposure" as the null case the department's methodology has to argue
against.
**SlopU adaptation:** an assigned first-week reading framed as a
department "philosophy of practice" text — used to justify why the
department insists on procedure at all, rather than treated as a case.

#### 9. "The Dunwich Horror" (H. P. Lovecraft, 1928) — Voorish Sign and Powder of Ibn-Ghazi

- **URL:** https://lovecraft.fandom.com/wiki/Necronomicon ; https://arkhamhorrorfiles.fandom.com/wiki/Powder_of_Ibn-Ghazi
  (fan wikis; primary text is public domain and widely mirrored, e.g. HathiTrust/Project Gutenberg-hosted collections)
- **Author/site:** H. P. Lovecraft (public domain in the US); fan wikis for
  secondary detail, unclear license
- **Language:** English
- **Week fit:** 3
- **Tags:** detection countermeasure, invisible threat, exacting recipe,
  forbidden-text lore

Within the story, an invisible monster can only be perceived by two
combined measures drawn from the (fictional) Necronomicon: the Voorish
Sign, a specific hand gesture/incantation, and the Powder of Ibn-Ghazi, a
compounded dust that renders an invisible thing visible for roughly ten
heartbeats when blown onto or over it. The powder is explicitly described
as requiring exact compounding from named ingredients — sloppy preparation
is implied to be why the ordinary person can't just use it. The protagonists
use the combination only once, at the climax, specifically to make the
creature visible long enough to identify and then banish it with a
separate dismissal ritual. Teachable concept: a countermeasure split into
two functions — *detection* (make the anomaly perceivable at all) and
*neutralisation* (a separate, later step) — rather than one ritual doing
both, a structurally useful two-stage model for a "how the department
actually handles a case" framework.
**SlopU adaptation:** the department's real countermeasure kit is
explicitly two-part (a detection reagent, a separate neutralisation
protocol) modeled on this split, with the fictional powder's "ten
heartbeats" limit reused as a stated timing constraint for the in-universe
tool.

#### 10. "The Painted Skin" (畫皮, Pu Songling, *Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio*, c.1740)

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Painted_Skin ;
  https://pressbooks.pub/chin330/chapter/the-painted-skin-critical-analysis/
- **Author/site:** Pu Songling (public domain; first English translation
  by Herbert A. Giles, 1880, also public domain); Reed College student
  course-press critical essay (Pressbooks, check institutional reuse terms
  before quoting directly)
- **Language:** English (translated from Chinese)
- **Week fit:** 3 — directly pairs with items 2 and 3
- **Tags:** talisman failure case, Taoist priest, partial protection,
  exorcism sequence

A scholar takes in a beautiful woman who is secretly a demon wearing a
painted human skin; a Taoist priest recognizes the enchantment on sight and
warns him, but the scholar dismisses it. After the scholar witnesses the
demon painting its disguise, the priest gives him a talisman to stick on
his door, which does repel the demon that night — but a fuller version of
the tale has the priest instead give a duster to hang on the bedroom door,
which the demon simply tears apart before killing the scholar. Only
afterward does the priest track and destroy the demon, forcing it out of
its skin with a wooden/peach-wood sword. This is the single richest
documented **failure case** found for this whole phase: a real,
citable narrative where a warding object works once, is escalated to a
different (weaker) object, and that object visibly fails, with a named
in-story reason (implied insufficiency of the object versus the threat) —
also useful for the audit's earlier point that the story pointedly
undercuts the priest's own authority by having non-magical human
resolve do more real work than his magic. Teachable concept: a
countermeasure system where an *object substitution* — not sloppy
procedure — is the failure point, a distinct failure category from the
Hoichi/omission case below.
**SlopU adaptation:** a department incident report modeled directly on
this substitution failure: field staff swap a certified ward for a
lesser stand-in and log it as equivalent, with fatal results — a case
study in "read the spec, don't improvise the materials."

#### 11. *The Blair Witch Project* (1999, dir. Myrick & Sánchez) — stick-figure markers

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blair_Witch_Project ;
  https://screenrant.com/blair-witch-project-movie-stick-figures-meaning/
- **Author/site:** Wikipedia (CC BY-SA); ScreenRant (standard copyright)
- **Language:** English
- **Week fit:** 1 or 2 (semiotics of a warning system, ahead of the week 3
  countermeasure unit)
- **Tags:** warning marker, ignored signal, escalating frequency, found
  footage

Production designer Ben Rock based the film's five-limbed "twana" figures
on a rune illustration from Nigel Pennick's *Magical Alphabets*. In-story,
three student filmmakers find these figures hung from trees with
increasing frequency the deeper they go, are told (by the filmmakers, in
interviews) that the symbols function as a warning, and disturb one anyway
by cutting it down for a closer look — after which their situation
worsens. The figures are not a countermeasure themselves; they are a
documented *signal system* whose entire "rule" is escalating density
correlating with escalating danger, and whose one actionable instruction
(leave; don't touch) is exactly the one the characters violate. Teachable
concept: distinguishing a warning/signage system from an active
countermeasure — useful groundwork before week 3's actual wards, since a
warning that is understood but ignored is a different failure mode from a
ward that is followed but insufficient (see item 10).
**SlopU adaptation:** the department's site markers ("do not remove, do
not photograph, report increasing density") reusing this exact escalating-
frequency-as-signal logic, with a stock case file of a field team that
ignores its own signage.

#### 12. "Hōichi the Earless" (耳無芳一の話, in Lafcadio Hearn's *Kwaidan*, 1904; filmed 1964, dir. Kobayashi)

- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoichi_the_Earless
- **Author/site:** Lafcadio Hearn (public domain); Wikipedia (CC BY-SA) for
  film/reception detail
- **Language:** English (Hearn's retelling of a Japanese legend)
- **Week fit:** 3 — pairs with item 5 (ofuda/Shinto-Buddhist practice) and
  directly parallels item 10 as a second failure case
- **Tags:** body-warding, sutra inscription, omission failure, exacting
  completeness

A blind biwa-playing priest's ward, Hōichi, is nightly summoned by a ghost
to perform for the dead. A temple priest protects him by inscribing the
entire Heart Sutra over his whole body in ink and instructing him to stay
silent and motionless — but the priest (or his assistant, depending on
retelling) omits Hōichi's ears. The ghosts return, can perceive only the
two unprotected ears floating where a head should be, and — reasoning the
rest of him must simply be invisible, not absent — tear the ears off as
proof of having completed their task. Hōichi survives, mutilated, precisely
because the *rest* of the warding worked. This is the cleanest documented
**omission failure case** across all sources gathered: total coverage
matters, and the countermeasure's own completeness (not its potency) is
the variable that fails. Teachable concept: a checklist/completeness
failure mode, distinct from Painted Skin's substitution failure and
Ringu's complicity "loophole" — three structurally different failure
categories from three traditions.
**SlopU adaptation:** a department safety bulletin titled after a redacted
incident, illustrating a countermeasure that was applied correctly but
incompletely — the department's stock cautionary tale for why checklists
have a mandatory sign-off on every line item, not just the risky-looking
ones.

### Rejected candidates (and why)

- **The Conjuring franchise / any Warren-case-derived horror media.**
  Rejected outright — these are explicitly tied to real named private
  individuals (the Warrens and the families involved), which the brief
  excludes regardless of how fictionalized the film treatment is.
- **Ring 2 (film) and other direct Sadako/Samara backstory sequels.**
  Rejected as redundant with item 6 — they expand mythology/backstory
  rather than adding a new rule or a new failure case, so they don't teach
  anything item 6 doesn't already cover for this course's purposes.
- **Salem witch trial procedural material (period "tests" for witchcraft).**
  Considered because it has real procedural detail (pin tests, water
  tests, etc.), but rejected — it is inseparable from a real historical
  incident with real named executed individuals, which the brief excludes
  even though the "test" procedures themselves are generic.
- **"Ghosts building a wall" (鬼打墙/鬼打牆) Chinese folk motif.**
  Considered as a sixth tradition item, but rejected as a standalone entry
  — it describes an affliction (getting lost in circles at night) rather
  than a protective countermeasure with steps, so it doesn't fit this
  phase's procedural-first brief. Kept only as a one-line aside inside
  item 3, since it reinforces the same "straight lines matter" logic.
- **General Necronomicon lore beyond the Dunwich Horror excerpt.**
  Rejected as a separate item — the broader fictional grimoire's lore is
  vast and mostly plot ornamentation; only the Voorish Sign/Powder of
  Ibn-Ghazi mechanic (item 9) has the specific procedural, testable rule
  this phase needs.
