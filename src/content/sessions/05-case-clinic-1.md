## Before the session

Consent form and buddy pairing from week 4 must be on file. Bring your log
template, a thermometer/hygrometer pair, and a nazar amulet from the
department's fieldwork kit.

## In the session

The case: Seminar Room 3 in the Arts Annex has sat empty since a
renovation bricked over its second fire exit, and facilities flagged it
"do not reassign" rather than update the floor plan. Entered alone, the
room reads as extending further than that floor plan allows — a second,
identical-seeming stretch of room beyond where the corridor wall should
be. Nobody has produced a log of what, if anything, is at the far end of
it, because nobody who has gone to look has been able to find their own
way back out.

This week's protocol is explicitly two stages, run separately and logged
separately:

1. **Diagnose.** Before the room counts as "live" for the case log, take a
   thermometer and hygrometer reading just inside the door and compare it
   against a baseline reading taken in the corridor. The extension reads
   measurably cooler and drier than the corridor outside — a real,
   loggable instrument signal, not an impression. A reading within normal
   range for the building means the case isn't live today; log that too.
2. **Respond.** Once the reading confirms the case is live, one observer
   enters while the other stays in the corridor. The rule is not
   optional: an occupant cannot find their own way back out, and only the
   observer who stayed outside, opening the door from that side, ends the
   observation. This is the case the buddy-system rule from week 4 was
   introduced for — corroboration and rescue are the same requirement
   here, not two separate justifications for the same paperwork.

Separately from both stages, log whether the nazar amulet clipped to the
inside door handle has cracked since the last cohort's visit. This is the
corpus's first real fieldwork test of a week-3 measure — logged honestly
as a data point to compare against the instrument reading, not asserted
to have done anything. A cracked nazar is not treated as confirmation, and
an intact one is not treated as a clean reading; both go in the log next
to whatever the thermometer says.

## Afterwards

Logs are collected into the course's running record of the case, which
every cohort adds to. Your own log, this week's classification call, and
both the diagnose/respond and nazar entries are raw material for the
Field Case File due in week 6.
