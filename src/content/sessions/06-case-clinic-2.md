## In the practical

The case: the west stairwell in the Design building occasionally returns an
observer to the floor they started on after descending or ascending thirteen
steps, rather than the floor the stairwell's geometry would predict. It does
not do this every time, or to every observer, or even necessarily to the same
observer twice in one week — which is most of why this course wants it
logged rather than explained.

Two additions to what earlier cohorts logged:

- **A non-visual signal.** When the effect is live, the stairwell's light
  fixtures dim below a stated wattage threshold — independently of
  whether anyone counts steps that day. Log the reading; it doesn't
  require a count to be worth recording.
- **The fourteen-step edge case.** Thirteen steps, counted correctly,
  returns you to your starting floor. Fourteen does not — it skips you
  down an extra floor instead, rather than simply failing to return you.
  This is why the course treats "thirteen steps" as a stated rule with a
  documented edge, not a round number that happens to recur.

One entry in the case's history is on record but not open for further
observation: **Incident SU-6-04**<span class="stamp">access restricted</span>,
an expedition that continued past a disengagement trigger. Access to that
flight has been restricted since, and no further detail is available to
this cohort — cite it as precedent, not as something you can go verify
yourself.

Same protocol as case clinic 1: buddy pairs, step counts logged aloud as
they happen rather than reconstructed afterwards from memory, disengagement
the moment either observer stops trusting the count.
