## In the session

Two traditions, presented back to back rather than one after the other, so
the comparison is the point rather than an afterthought:

- **Western threshold and boundary practice** — salt lines, iron across an
  opening, rites performed at the moment of crossing. Read as claims about
  *where* containment holds (the boundary itself) and *when* it needs
  reinforcing (at transition).
- **Chinese feng shui boundary theory and fú talismans** — orientation and
  flow across a space, talismans placed at points the theory predicts are
  structurally weak. Read as claims about *why* a boundary holds where it
  does, not only where to draw one.

The department's interest is where these converge despite no documented
contact between the two traditions: both put the emphasis on **transitions**
— doorways, corners, points where a space changes — rather than the open
middle of a room. That convergence, not either tradition's internal logic, is
the countermeasure principle the department actually uses in the field.
