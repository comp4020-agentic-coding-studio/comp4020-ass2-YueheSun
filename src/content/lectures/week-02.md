## Outline

- the four categories restated as questions to ask of a case, not labels to
  memorise: does the behaviour hold steady (persistent), repeat on a
  pattern (recurring), trigger on an action (responsive), or sit inactive
  until then (dormant) — and why a case can answer yes to more than one
- worked application: an object with a genuinely affectionate, unchanging
  baseline that, rarely and only while unobserved, does something else
  entirely — classified persistent *and* dormant at once, two tracked
  channels rather than one forced fit. Full case held back for week 7;
  this week only trains the classification move, not the story behind it
- why this week has no case of its own: no sourced material was written
  specifically for week 2, and this course does not invent one to fill the
  slot — the scheme is taught here as a tool to be tested against cases
  you will meet properly from week 5 onward, not demonstrated on a
  placeholder
- what carries forward: this vocabulary is what week 3's traditions get
  read through, and what your week 5–6 case files are marked against

## A note on this week's content

This lecture is intentionally thin on new case material, for a stated
reason rather than by oversight: the corpus this course draws from has
real, sourced content for every other week, but nothing written for week 2
specifically. Rather than pad the gap with an invented incident, the week
teaches the classification method itself — the same non-exclusive-category
move the practical introduces — and holds its first real test for week 7,
where the persistent-plus-dormant case gets its full write-up.
