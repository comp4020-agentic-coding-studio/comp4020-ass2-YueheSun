## Outline

- where this week's three rules actually come from: paranormal-
  investigation safety guidance, urban-exploration ethics, and academic
  fieldwork risk-assessment policy — three real traditions that
  independently converge on the same core moves (never alone, a mapped
  walkthrough first, a named outside contact with a return-time deadline)
- the one addition academic risk-assessment guidance brings that the other
  two don't: a plan for psychological distress, not just physical injury —
  this course's own disengagement rule is closer to that framing than to a
  ghost-hunting show's dramatic "get out now"
- consent as a site-selection filter: no site is observed without the
  course's sign-off that it's currently sanctioned, and nobody is placed
  anywhere they wouldn't otherwise go as an ordinary part of campus life
- why the buddy system isn't just "two is safer than one": previewed with
  next week's first case, where a trapped observer cannot self-extract —
  only a second person outside the site can end the observation. The rule
  stops being abstract the moment corroboration and rescue turn out to be
  the same requirement
- the disengagement rule's trigger is procedural, not a judgment call made
  under pressure: "no longer feels able to leave at will" ends the
  observation immediately, with nothing left to argue about in the moment
