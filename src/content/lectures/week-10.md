## Outline

- a framework earns space in this phase only if it makes a claim precise
  enough to generate a possible counterexample. Cosmic prisoner theory and
  shadow domain theory, restated as precisely as their own popular accounts
  allow: what would a case have to do to break either one? Neither answer
  survives being asked out loud: not because the ideas are wrong, but
  because neither is stated precisely enough to be wrong
- Freud's uncanny (1919): dread as something familiar returning in
  disguise, not something foreign arriving. The week-1 forum thread
  (alumni "remembering" a broadcast that never aired, each retelling
  more specific and more wrong than the last) is this course's own case
  for it: not a foreign intrusion, a memory turning unfamiliar from the
  inside
- Fisher's weird and eerie (2016): the eerie as "a presence where there
  should be nothing" rather than a monster where there should be
  something. This course's own recording-hypothesis case files (a
  disused building treated as though its fabric could store and replay
  a past event) are a direct, deliberately unglamorous worked example
- Turner's liminality (1969): anomalous behaviour clustering at
  thresholds and transitional states, not at random. Week 3's covered
  mirrors during a household's mourning week are liminality by another
  name: a real, still-practiced custom treating a threshold moment as
  the point of actual vulnerability
- the course's actual thesis, stated outright rather than implied: these
  three frameworks share one move (taking something ordinary and
  showing the strangeness already inside it), not a competing claim
  about one underlying cause. That is also why none of the three is
  presented as this course's position; they're read the same way
  cosmic prisoner and shadow domain theory were, for what each predicts
  and what would break it

## After this lecture
