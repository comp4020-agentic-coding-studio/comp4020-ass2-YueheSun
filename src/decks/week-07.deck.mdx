# Regional case survey

Week 7 — desk study begins

## No fieldwork this week

The phase changes from on-campus observation to documented cases. Nothing
here was witnessed by this course directly — everything is read, sourced,
and weighed against how well it survives that reading.

---

## Three requirements before a case counts

1. **Sourcing** — does it name where it came from?
2. **Behaviour, not atmosphere** — does it describe what happened, not
   just how it felt?
3. **Corroboration** — is there more than one account behind it?

A striking case that fails any of these still gets read. It just doesn't
get weight in a comparison.

# Three ways to fail sourcing without looking fake

Not degrees of the same problem — three distinct failure modes.

---

## Failure mode 1: official-sounding, corroborated by nothing

- **The case** — facility-scale, with a published containment procedure
- **What audit found** — no demonstrated causal relationship between the
  procedure and the anomaly's actual behaviour
- **The lesson** — a procedure can read as authoritative while remaining
  pure narrative, with no working countermeasure behind it

---

## Failure mode 2: structurally uncorroborable by design

- **The case** — its own mechanism is "everyone who could confirm it
  either passed it on or is compromised"
- **The problem** — no position exists from which an independent witness
  could ever check it
- **The call** — read for method, never weighted in a comparison

---

## Failure mode 3: no evidence at any point in its history

- **The case** — a device supposedly appeared, caused documented harm,
  then was removed by unmarked agents
- **The check** — no unit, registration, or contemporary account has ever
  surfaced, at any stage
- **The distinction** — not "hard to verify now": nothing to verify
  existed in the first place

---

## Case-format literacy

The same three questions apply whether a case arrives as a text write-up
or a video/software claim — the format changes what a fake looks like,
not whether the test still applies.

- **Text case:** an object motionless only under continuous direct
  observation by two or more witnesses, contact injuries reported
  exclusively during momentary lapses.
- **Video/software case:** a claimed behaviour on period hardware — the
  sourcing question here is whether that behaviour is even *possible* on
  the hardware claimed; footage looking real isn't sufficient on its own.

## The week-2 reveal

Held back on purpose since week 2: an object with a genuinely
affectionate, unchanging baseline that, rarely and only while unobserved,
does something else entirely — scavenged materials, crude and violent,
then nothing again. Classified persistent *and* dormant, two tracked
channels, not one forced fit.

---

## Why it was held back

Week 2 taught the classification move on this case without the story
behind it, on purpose. You now have the sourcing method too — read this
case for what it corroborates, past how striking it looks.

---

## A countermeasure that made things worse

A secondhand cartridge, an unfamiliar save file, escalating glitches —
and the standard fix, wiping and resetting it, is the documented action
that made it worse. The obvious fix is itself an action, not a neutral
default.

---

## Halloween candy tampering: a legend's effect as data

Newspaper survey, four papers, 1958–84: 76 reported incidents, injuries
in only 20, no verified stranger-poisoning deaths — and real defensive
behaviour every October regardless. The legend's content doesn't hold
up. Its effect on real behaviour is itself worth treating as data.
