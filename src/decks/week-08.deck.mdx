# The international corpus

Week 8 — global case survey

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## Primary source data, not folklore to filter out

A widely-circulated account isn't discounted for being widely
circulated. It's often exactly the case with the deepest sourcing trail
to actually check.

---

## Case one: Bloody Mary

Alone, in a darkened bathroom, a participant repeats a name a fixed
number of times facing a mirror. A spectral face is supposed to appear.

An ordinary household bathroom mirror — nothing exotic required.

---

## The scholarship's own finding

What participants frequently report is their own reflection, reframed.
Last week's Freud citation, made concrete: not a foreign intrusion,
something familiar returning in disguise.

---

## Case two: Hanako-san

A schoolgirl ghost said to haunt one specific stall of a school
bathroom. Knock three times, ask if she's there.

---

## What travels, and what doesn't

Hanako's backstory varies freely across retellings. The ritual's
counted mechanics — which stall, how many knocks — stay stable. That's
backwards from most ghost lore, where biography is stable and method
drifts.

---

# Two countries. No contact. Same structure.

A responsive ghost bound to an institutional fixture, summoned by a
scripted count. Independently documented. No plausible line of
transmission between the sources.

---

## Why the pairing matters more than either case alone

Neither case is unusual by itself. The pairing is the department's
actual comparative method: convergence with no contact is evidence
worth weighing.

---

## A sourcing-integrity check: the Charlie Charlie Challenge

Reported everywhere in 2015 as an authentic named demon game. No such
demon, by that name, was ever found by anyone who looked.

---

## What was actually there

A real, older, broader Spanish-language pencil-balancing game family —
a real tradition wearing a fabricated brand name and origin story
picked up during viral spread.
