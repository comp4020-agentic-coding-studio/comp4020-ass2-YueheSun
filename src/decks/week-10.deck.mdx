# Competing frameworks

Week 10 — literature synthesis

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## What would have to be true to break this?

Every framework this week is read for one thing: state its claim
precisely enough to say what observation would falsify it. Most popular
accounts can't clear that bar as written.

---

## Cosmic prisoner theory, stated as precisely as it gets

**Claim:** anomalous sites are containment failures around something
already contained elsewhere, leaking rather than originating locally.

**What would break it:** a case with no plausible elsewhere to leak
from. Popular accounts never specify what that elsewhere is, so this
can't actually be asked.

---

## Shadow domain theory, stated as precisely as it gets

**Claim:** anomalous behaviour is bleed-through from an adjacent,
structurally similar domain, rather than a property of the site itself.

**What would break it:** a case where the site's own history explains
the behaviour better than any adjacent domain. Popular accounts never
say what "structurally similar" would have to mean for that comparison
to be testable.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Neither idea is wrong. Neither is stated precisely enough to be wrong.

---

## Freud's uncanny (1919)

Dread as something familiar returning in disguise, not something
foreign arriving. The frightening thing was never the stranger at the
door — it's the thing you already knew, seen from an angle that makes
it unrecognizable.

---

## The week-1 case, reread

A forum thread, three years old: alumni "remembering" a broadcast that
never aired, each retelling more specific and more wrong than the last.
Not an intrusion. A memory turning strange from the inside — exactly
Freud's shape, months before this course named it.

---

## Fisher's weird and eerie (2016)

The eerie: **a presence where there should be nothing** — not a monster
where there should be something. Failure of absence, not failure of
safety.

---

## A recording-hypothesis case file

A disused campus building, treated as though its own fabric could store
and play back a past event — no occult object, no entity, just an
unremarkable structure and an unglamorous hypothesis about what it retains.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> A presence where there should be nothing.

---

## Turner's liminality (1969)

Anomalous behaviour clusters at thresholds and transitions — doorways,
corners, mirrors, the week between a death and its burial — not at
random points in the rest of space and time.

---

## A real custom, already seen in week 3

Covering every mirror in a household during its mourning week. Multiple
explanations given side by side, one of them centuries older than the
other — a threshold moment treated as the actual point of vulnerability,
not decoration around it.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## The course's actual thesis

Freud, Fisher, and Turner don't converge on one cause. They converge on
one move: take something ordinary, and show the strangeness that was
already inside it. That move — not a hidden mechanism — is what this
course is actually teaching.
