# Writing up a case file

Week 6 — Field Case File due

---

{/* _class: banner */}

## A log isn't a case file

A case file states a classification and defends it against the log. A
log is raw material; a case file is an argument.

---

## Worked review: the sub-basement stacks

Documentation-rigor pass: does the log let a second observer navigate
the stacks as you found them, without you there to explain it? That's
the actual bar — not whether the write-up reads well.

---

## Worked review: the 13-step stairwell

Safety-protocol adherence shows up here in the write-up, not only in the
field: was the buddy present for the full count, and did either observer
invoke disengagement — and if not, why not?

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Analytical quality means engagement, not tidy prose

Apply week 2's classification scheme and defend the call against the
log. A well-written paragraph that skips this still fails the criterion.

---

## A sample log entry

> Observer [redacted], buddy-paired, west stairwell, descending. Counted
> thirteen steps aloud with buddy confirming. Arrived at starting floor.
> Re-ran ascending: sixteen steps, arrived one floor above starting
> point as geometry predicts. No disengagement invoked. Provisional
> classification: responsive.

---

## Why this entry works

Count and confirmation are stated separately, and the classification is
marked provisional rather than final. Redacting the observer's identity
costs nothing a marker needs: what happened, and what it's classified as.
