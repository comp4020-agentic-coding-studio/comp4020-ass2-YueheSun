# Classification as a method

Week 2 — before the scheme meets a real case

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## A scheme earns its place by what it can classify

Not by a good story about where it came from. Every retired scheme in
this course's history is described by the structural gap it failed to
accommodate, no invented "case zero" required.

---

## Three schemes that broke

1. **Hostile / benign** — a single axis, failed the first time a case
   read as both in the same week
2. **Indoor / outdoor / liminal** — a spatial scheme, couldn't place a
   phenomenon that changed category between observations
3. **The current scheme** — classifies by *behaviour under observation*,
   not appearance or location

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# Four questions, not four boxes

Persistent. Recurring. Responsive. Dormant. Each is a question you ask of
a case, never a label you file it under.

---

## Ask each case four questions

- does the behaviour hold **steady** every time it's observed? →
  persistent
- does it **repeat on a pattern** you can predict? → recurring
- does it **trigger on an action** taken by the observer? → responsive
- does it sit **inactive** until one of the above happens? → dormant

A case can answer yes to more than one.

## Membership is non-exclusive, on purpose

The old hostile/benign scale forced a single fit. This scheme doesn't:
a case can be tracked on two channels at once, and the classification is
more accurate for it, no less rigorous.

---

## Worked example, held for week 7

An object with a consistently affectionate, entirely benign baseline
behaviour that, rarely and only while unobserved, does something else
altogether. Classified **persistent** *and* **dormant** at the same time:
two tracked channels on one case file, no forced choice required. Full
write-up in week 7; this week only trains the move.
